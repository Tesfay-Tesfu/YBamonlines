📋 For parents & tutors: how this helps students stop counting on fingers

Why fingers? Finger counting means a fact isn't stored in memory yet, so the student rebuilds it every time. That's slow and tiring, especially in multi-step problems like fractions. The goal is fluency: accurate, fast (about 3 seconds) and flexible.

How the app works:

  • Strategy Lab: each fact is linked to a fact the student already knows (×2, ×5, ×10). Example: 7 × 8 = 4 × 7 + 4 × 7, and 7 × 6 = 5 × 7 + 1 × 7. This replaces counting with reasoning.
  • Practice is adaptive. Facts that are new, wrong or slow come back more often. A wrong answer shows the picture, and the fact returns a few questions later.
  • Speed Run compares students only to their own best score. It's never a pass/fail test.

Suggested order (one family per week; division goes with its multiplication family):
Week 1: ×2, ×5, ×10 · Week 2: ×3, ×4 · Week 3: ×9, ×6 · Week 4: ×7, ×8 · Week 5: ×11, ×12 and mixed review.

Tips: 10 minutes a day is better than 1 hour once a week. Praise strategies (“You used 5 × 8!”), not just speed. If they reach for their fingers, ask: “Which fact you already know is close?”

To reset a student’s progress, use the student’s page in AMtutorTools (tutors and admins).
